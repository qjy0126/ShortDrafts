Use case

Product and promo

Paste a brief and get a short ad script, titles, and caption variants — then shoot the thing on your table.

By Elena Voss · Published Sep 7, 2026

What to paste

A product name, who it is for, one proof, and a price or offer if you have one. Optional: the objection you always hear. The pack writes a spoken ad, titles, and a caption — not a brand film.

“Matte-black daily sneaker, $89, for people who walk 8k steps and hate breaking in leather” is a brief. “Promote my brand” is not.

How to shoot it

You still hold the product. Use the script as a talking-head pass, or as voiceover over hands, unbox, and B-roll. One offer per clip. If you have a code, say it once at the end — not in the hook.

  • Keep the hook on the outcome, not the logo
  • Show the product in the first three seconds if the script mentions it
  • Regenerate titles when you need a search-led variant for Shorts

What this will not do

It will not stitch stock, clone a voice, or guarantee a sale. Claims about medical results, income, or guaranteed outcomes do not belong in a 30-second promo here. Copy the words, then shoot what is actually on the table.

What the platforms actually say

A promo pack is advertising copy. If you were paid, given the product, or related to the brand, U.S. endorsement rules expect that connection to be obvious in the clip or caption. ShortDrafts will not write a fake “not a paid ad” line to hide a deal.

YouTube still wants a title of at most 100 characters when you upload a Short. Use one of the pack titles; do not paste a paragraph into that field. Show the product in the first seconds if the script names it.

Sources

If there is a connection between an endorser and the seller that consumers would not expect, that connection should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously.

— U.S. Federal Trade Commission, Endorsement Guides
