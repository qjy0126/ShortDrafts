Why every pack leads with hooks

The first three seconds are the job. Slow intros get skipped.

By Alex Brennan · Published Sep 7, 2026

The three rules

A Short lives or dies before the viewer decides to stay. Packs are written that way on purpose.

  • Open on a claim, a tension, or a picture — not a greeting
  • Make the next line earn the last one
  • Land a CTA that a real person would actually say

What you are working with

Each pack includes several hook options so you can test more than one cold open on the same idea. If a hook feels like a clickbait lie, skip it. The rest of the script should still make sense without it.

After you pick a hook

Read the full script out loud. If you cannot say it in one breath-friendly pass, cut it before you shoot. The generator drafts. You keep the final line.

What the platforms actually say

YouTube now lets creators request in-app feedback on a Short’s hook and pacing after they record. That is a post-shoot check. The pack still leads with several hook lines so you choose a cold open before the camera rolls.

A greeting wastes the first three seconds. Open on a claim, a tension, or a picture. If a hook oversells the script, throw the hook away and keep the rest.
